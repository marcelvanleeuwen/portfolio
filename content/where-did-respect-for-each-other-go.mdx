# Where Did Respect for Each Other Go?

Sometimes I look at the world around me and wonder what happened to basic respect for other people. Maybe I am getting older and notice it more. Maybe social media makes bad behaviour more visible than it was in the past. 

Bullying often starts at school. Someone looks different, talks differently, has different interests or simply does not fit into the group. That can already be enough to become a target. We often think that children will grow out of this behaviour. That they will become adults, learn from life and start treating other people with more respect.

Unfortunately, not everyone does. Some people grow older, but never really grow up. The school playground is replaced by the workplace, the street, social media or the internet. Making fun of someone, insulting someone, excluding someone or trying to make another person feel small somehow becomes normal behaviour. But it should never be normal.

Today almost everyone has a smartphone in their pocket. When something happens, it sometimes feels like the first reaction is no longer “Can I help?” Instead, people take out their phones. There is a fight? Film it. There is an accident? Film it. Someone is injured or lying on the ground? Film it. And before you know it, the video is shared on social media, WhatsApp groups or websites like Dumpert.

Why? For attention, likes or views? Have we really reached a point where filming someone's worst moment is more important than helping that person? The person lying on the ground is not content. That is a human being. Someone's son or daughter, father or mother, partner, friend or colleague. 

People should also understand that you cannot simply film and publish everything without thinking about privacy and possible legal consequences. Having a smartphone does not mean that everything you see should become content for the internet. Sometimes the most respectful thing you can do is simply put your phone away and help.

You can see the same attitude in everyday life. You are riding your bicycle and someone does not give you right of way. Instead of saying sorry, you get the middle finger. Two people are cycling next to each other and someone wants to pass, but they do not move aside. Is giving someone a little space really so difficult?

You are waiting at a red traffic light in your car. Your light turns green and you start driving. But a car coming from the other direction decides to quickly drive through the red light. You have to brake to avoid hitting the car. At the same time, a scooter that also has a green light continues driving. The scooter crashes into the car that went through the red light and ends up on its hood.

What worries me is the attitude we seem to see more and more: me first. My time is more important. My opinion is more important. My road is more important. My phone, likes and views are more important.

Ask yourself one simple question: how would I want to be treated if I were the other person?

We do not have to agree with everyone and we do not have to like everyone. We can have different opinions, different backgrounds and completely different lives. But we can still treat each other with respect. Respect costs nothing. Kindness costs nothing. 

If bullying becomes normal, if filming becomes more important than helping, if a few seconds of our own time become more important than someone else's safety, and if we only think about ourselves, then maybe we should seriously ask ourselves:

How did we let society fall this far?
